# Inn Game Concept

## Current Focus

The current design target is a vertical slice that is also day 1 of the full game.

The player is an inn manager. Their job is to keep the inn running, profitable, and respected while juggling competing responsibilities. The core experience is not doing tasks in isolation, but choosing what matters most under pressure.

## Core Fantasy

Keep a busy inn functional, profitable, and respected despite constant demands and limited time.

The player should feel like the one person holding the operation together.

## Core Design Pillars

- Prioritization under pressure is the main skill.
- Decisions should create practical consequences, not just abstract bonuses.
- Tutorialization should happen through real play, not heavy explanation.
- The inn should feel lived in, specific, and operationally messy.

## Vertical Slice Goal

Prove that running an inn for a single day is fun because the player must balance service, labor, and judgment while protecting both profit and reputation.

## Day 1 Framing

Day 1 should teach the player how the game works without feeling like a detached tutorial.

The intended model is a "learn by doing" opening. Early situations should be simple, legible, and believable, with each one introducing a new layer of responsibility.

Day 1 can be abnormally bad as long as the escalation is readable. The player should feel overwhelmed by stacked operational problems, but still understand why each problem is happening and what tradeoff each response creates.

The tone target is that the inn is survivable, but fragile. Friday the 13th, a storm, staffing instability, and a facility problem can all push the day into crisis without making it feel random.

## Early Mechanical Focus

- Guest check-in
- Walk-in guest handling
- Room assignment
- Room readiness
- Damaged or unavailable rooms
- Cleanup caused by guest traffic and bad weather
- Hands-on inn work by the manager
- Tradeoffs between convenience, reputation, and effort
