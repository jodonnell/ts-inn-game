# Open Questions

## Current Questions

- Is day 1 meant to cover a full inn day, or a smaller playable slice such as the early morning through breakfast?
- Should the player be able to turn the walk-in away outright, or should the game strongly steer them into making room somehow?
- How much manual leak response or room triage should the player do before it starts to feel like busywork?
- How much cleanup from muddy footprints or tracked-in water should be manual before it starts to feel repetitive?
- Is the leaking room fully unusable, temporarily salvageable, or sellable at reduced quality?
- What is the first real interruption after the walk-in and leak decision is resolved?
- What stats or outcomes matter at the end of day 1: profit, reputation, guest satisfaction, staff morale, or something else?
- How hands-on is the manager throughout the game versus later delegation to staff?

## Things We Have Effectively Chosen

- The vertical slice should also be day 1 of the full game.
- Day 1 is allowed to be abnormally bad as long as the escalation is readable.
- The opening begins with a bell ringing at 5:32 AM.
- The day is Friday the 13th.
- A storm hit overnight.
- The manager wakes in a bedroom connected directly to the front desk area.
- One employee has called in sick before the player reaches the desk.
- One employee has quit by text with no notice before the player reaches the desk.
- The first guest interaction is a walk-in check-in.
- The first task should not be interrupted.
- The first meaningful choice is tied to room assignment under bad conditions.
- The nicest room has a leak, so the best room on paper may not be sellable.
- The storm can create muddy footprints and tracked-in water as an immediate cleanup problem.
